## Deliverables

Render (src/render)

- constants.ts: output geometry, encoder settings, transition floor, caption
  safe area and type size, QC tolerances, timeouts. One file so the graph, the
  QC thresholds and the tests cannot drift apart.
- plan.ts: buildRenderPlan turns the timing document's narration spans into
  clip frames, crossfade boundaries, chapter track and total duration. Pure and
  integer-frame based, so chapter boundaries and xfade offsets are the same
  numbers. Gaps between scenes are closed forward onto the earlier scene (a
  pause is silence over the previous picture), overlaps are refused, and a
  scene under 0.6 s is downgraded to a one-frame cut per section 6.3.
- graph.ts: the FFmpeg argv and one filter_complex string — cover-scale with
  lanczos, centre crop, conservative unsharp, zoompan per camera_move (capped at
  1.2x), xfade chain or concat when transitions are off, ASS burn-in, narration
  delayed by the lead and padded, a silent music slot mixed at zero so the
  level structure is real, and the chapter metadata input.
- ffmpeg.ts: FfmpegRunner port plus ProcessFfmpegRunner. Timeout, abort, stderr
  tail, -progress parsing, peak RSS from /proc VmHWM, ffprobe parsing, and the
  two signalstats scans (YAVG per frame, YMAX per region). Pure parsers are
  exported so they are testable without a child process.
- captions.ts: word timings to cues (2 lines, width-aware, 30 chars/line at
  48 px type), ASS with margins derived from the safe area plus outline and
  shadow, and .srt/.vtt generated from the same cue list.
- render.ts: writes captions/chapters, runs FFmpeg once, returns the artefacts,
  the manifest and the measured cost.
- qc.ts: the six automated assertions of R-010-2...7 against the delivered
  file, plus the isolated caption burn-in scan.
- stage.ts: the RE stage. Resolves the timing document and scene images,
  renders in a temp dir, stores video/captions/ASS/manifest/benchmark as
  content-addressed assets linked through asset_ref, moves the stage to done,
  the project to QC, and parks the video in NEEDS_ATTENTION on failure.
- qc-stage.ts: the QC stage. Re-reads the delivered MP4 and the exact ASS that
  produced it, runs the six checks, stores the report, and on pass opens the FA
  gate (awaiting_approval) without approving anything.
- fake-ffmpeg.ts: deterministic fake used by the unit and DB suites.
- benchmark.ts and benchmark-cli.ts: the Milestone A benchmark and the fixture
  generator behind evidence/benchmark/.

Export (src/export)

- package.ts: the five-file package (MP4, .srt, .vtt, metadata.json,
  sources.json), its naming, hashes and both JSON documents.
- stage.ts: the EX stage. Refuses without an FA approve decision, refuses a QC
  report that is not pass, takes title/description/hashtags from the approved
  script revision, copies sourced claims with their URLs (creative series
  record none, R-027-4) and writes package-manifest.json tying the five files
  to the approval.

Tests: tests/helpers/render-fixtures.ts plus ten suites, 93 tests
(see "Commands run" for the per-file split).

## Render budget (CTO risk 2)

Measured with src/render/benchmark-cli.ts: 17 scenes, 2243 frames, 74.767 s of
narration, 1080x1920 at 30 fps, libx264 medium crf 20, AAC 128k, single
FFmpeg invocation, QC run on the delivered file in the same process.

| source                             | wall    | realtime factor | peak RSS | QC   |
| ---------------------------------- | ------- | --------------- | -------- | ---- |
| 1K (768x1376 stills scaled 1.406x) | 200.5 s | 2.68x           | 1066 MB  | pass |
| 2K (1536x2752 stills)              | 206.3 s | 2.76x           | 1257 MB  | pass |

Both are inside the stated 2 GB / ~5 +/- 3 min envelope, with the caveat below.

Host: AMD Ryzen Embedded R1600, 4 cores, Linux container, 19.9 GB RAM. The CPU
model is the DS923+ CPU, but this is not the NAS: no DSM, no NAS memory
pressure, no network storage for the images. The card's exit criterion is a run
whose host label says DS923+, and this run deliberately says
"dev host (Linux container, not DS923+)" rather than claiming a NAS number.
To close it, run in the worker image on the NAS:

    tsx src/render/benchmark-cli.ts --scenes 17 --duration 75 --resolution 1K \
      --out /volume1/reels/benchmark --json /volume1/reels/benchmark/1k.json \
      --label "DS923+ (DSM 7.2, 8 GB)"

The CLI writes the record, the MP4 and a caption still; it exits non-zero if QC
fails.

Reading of the two runs: 2K buys +3% wall and +18% peak RSS (1257 MB against
1066 MB) for a background the viewer sees through 0.3 s dissolves. On a NAS
with 8 GB shared with DSM and the database, that headroom is the budget that
matters, so the 1K default in PRD section 10 stands, now with numbers.

## Caption legibility at 1.406x (CTO risk 6)

Artifacts (open these, do not trust the summary):

- evidence/benchmark/caption-1k.png, caption-2k.png — the same timestamp
  (5.2 s) from the 1K and 2K renders, full 1080x1920 frame.
- evidence/benchmark/caption-crop-1k.png, caption-crop-2k.png — the identical
  918x420 caption region, 1:1 pixels, for a side-by-side eyeball.

Measured on those frames (Laplacian variance, higher = sharper):

| metric                      | 1K     | 2K     |
| --------------------------- | ------ | ------ |
| caption band sharpness      | 1241.1 | 1304.3 |
| background sharpness        | 53.1   | 65.1   |
| caption band mean luma      | 125.69 | 125.84 |
| glyph pixels above 200 luma | 69341  | 69741  |

Reading: the glyphs are statistically the same artefact in both renders
(within 5% sharpness, 0.6% ink coverage) because captions are burned at output
resolution after the upscale, so a 1K source cannot blur them. The 2K source
only sharpens the background, by about 22% Laplacian variance.

Honest limitation: the fixture images are testsrc2 patterns, not comic art, so
this is a sharpness measurement, not the operator's legibility verdict the CTO
risk asks for. The artifacts are produced so that verdict can be made on real
scenes without re-running anything but the fixture generator.

## Decisions worth review

1. asset_ref is the read path, not asset.project_id. content_sha256 is unique
   per blob (R-026), so a shared asset keeps the first project's provenance and
   the second project only gets an asset_ref link. QC and EX therefore resolve
   "this project's newest video / sidecar / report" by joining asset_ref and
   ordering on the link's created_at. A reader that filters on
   asset.project_id silently finds nothing on the second project — that is what
   the first EX test caught.

2. The export package reuses the render's blobs. Inserting the MP4/.srt/.vtt
   again cannot create rows (unique content_sha256), so EX adds metadata.json,
   sources.json and package-manifest.json, and the manifest names all five
   files with hashes and the approval id. If the reviewer expects five EX asset
   rows specifically, this needs a ruling, because the schema forbids it.

3. Captions are 48 px with a 30-character line budget. The design's "~42
   chars/line" is an upper bound; at 48 px, 42 characters overflow the 918 px
   safe width. 30 chars keeps ink inside by construction and the scan proves it.

4. QC's caption check burns the same ASS over black and scans four regions
   outside [54,1026]x[96,1824]. It proves placement, not glyph legibility — no
   OCR runs on the delivered frames, as TECH_DESIGN section 6.7 states.

5. RE/QC/EX write no job_attempt rows. They make no provider call and incur no
   charge, and the attempt table's shape is a provider-call ledger (R-012).
   Their execution cost goes into the benchmark asset instead.
